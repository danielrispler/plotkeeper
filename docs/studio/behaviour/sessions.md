# The sessions

## The session list

On the right, the session list: every session (Claude Code, agy and Codex), one line each with the agent's logo, its name and its state, grouped by repository, a task's worktree under its main checkout's, and, inside a repository, by state with "needs you" first. The list holds what Home showed (#382): a row that needs you adds a second line, the open question's first line, else who asks (a plan, a permission, or a subagent: its name and "asks"); a running row its Intention, or its current step until the Intention is known; an unseen row (your turn or idle, not landed) its last turn's Headline, which updates when the turn's summary is stored. No other row has a second line. A second line is one line, muted, at 11.5 px, cut with an ellipsis. **Cut text reads in full in place** (spec #582): a row's name, token and second line, like every text the layout cuts, keep their whole text in the page (find-in-page, copy and screen readers get all of it), and on hover, or focus on the control that holds them, the cut line scrolls its hidden end into view inside its own box (`cut()` in `ui/dom.ts`, the `.cut` class). A line that fits stays still, and with reduced motion asked for nothing moves. A text the layout shortens (a path) or that can be very long (a prompt) has a hint holding it whole instead. A row's hint carries the identity of a prompt-named session. The selected row adds how long it has waited ("Bash cargo test · 3m"); a session whose last step is a wake-up reads "worker … returned" until its next step. The agent's logo is 12 px in one muted tone; colour in the list is the "Needs you" pill only. Hovering a session's row makes it the spotlit session on the map: its footprint, its chip on its latest turn's parts, and the other session badges dimmed ([The Map view](map.md), "Session badges"). A session row, like every button and link of the session list and the top bar, explains itself in a **hint** (spec #582), a styled hover that replaces the native `title`: it shows when the pointer rests on the control for half a second, or when Tab reaches it, at once when it opens among neighbouring controls; it sits above the control and flips below when the window's top is in the way, stays open while the pointer is on it, and closes on Esc. A row's hint reads its name and state, and adds that it touched no part in the last 7 days when it did not; it is built when the row is drawn, and the sidebar is drawn again when the map places the sessions. A hint that adds to the control's name is its accessible description; one that only repeats it adds nothing. `hint(trigger, text)` in `ui/dom.ts` makes one; every other enabled button and link of the page (the map and its plates, the part sheet, the brief, the drawer, history, the tray, runs, pre-flight, ratify, the story, ask, the palette, writers, notes, evidence, the toasts and their inner buttons, and the buttons of the page's HTML) has one the same way, and a link in a rendered reply too; summaries, fields, disabled controls, cut text, marks and dots keep their `title` until the spec's later tickets move them.

"Clear landed", quiet under "History · N", shows only while a landed session is in the list (#244, #382). It hides every landed row at once, ended ones included, from the list and the strip, and shows a toast, "Cleared N landed", with Undo for 8 seconds. Undo brings the rows back and nothing was deleted. When the 8 seconds pass, or the app quits inside them, each hidden session goes through Delete: its row drops, and its cards stay. The rows stay hidden until the sessions leave the list, so they never flash back before the delete lands, and a Clear in that gap does not commit them twice; a delete that fails brings its row back. A reload of the page forgets a pending clear. A session that stops being landed inside the window is left alone. The server hears each session until its delete is written, so a quit while the deletes are in flight still commits them. A second Clear inside the window adds its rows and restarts the 8 seconds.

Idle sessions collapse at the bottom; ended ones are in History, from the "History · N" link at the list's end or the palette's "Show history". The rest of the page is the map of a project (see [The Map view](map.md#the-map-view), #386): the session you click opens its story (see [The story rail](rail.md)) in the left panel over its project's map, and History, a turn's whole diff, a subagent's log and Settings open in the same panel. Only your click changes the session shown.

The palette (⌘⇧P) lists every action with its keys, filtered by what you type; ↑ and ↓ move the active row, which a screen reader announces, as the filter is a combobox over a listbox of options, and Enter or a click runs one. It is a native popover with a visible × (named "Close"): Esc, a click outside it, its × or ⌘⇧P again close it, and every close puts the focus back where it was before it opened (#458).

A session is running from a prompt until its turn ends; needs you while it waits on your permission, answer or plan approval, or on a command auto mode blocked until the session moves on (see [Blocked commands](cards.md#blocked-commands)); your turn when its turn ended without asking you, or it started and has had no prompt yet; idle after 2 hours at your turn with no events, until its next event; ended from its `SessionEnd` until it starts again under its id. A session also ends when its agent is gone (#330): the hook records `studio_pid` on each event, the pid of its parent, or its parent's parent when that is a shell (`sh`, `bash`, `zsh`, `dash`, `fish`), and agy's wrapper passes its own parent as `PLOTKEEPER_AGENT_PID`, which wins. About every 30 seconds the server checks each session that has not ended and has a pid (a signal 0 to it, which sends nothing), and appends a `SessionEnd` with reason `process_gone` for each whose process is gone, so a Ctrl+C or a closed herdr pane ends it within about 30 seconds. The lines the server writes itself (this `SessionEnd`, a closed terminal's, an interrupted turn's `StudioInterrupted`) are shaped like Claude Code's whatever the session's agent, so they never change its agent: an agy session ended or interrupted this way keeps agy's logo and transcript reader, and a restart replays it the same (#352). A session with no pid never ends this way: with no events for 2 hours, in a turn or waiting on Daniel as well as at your turn, it goes idle and its row says "Idle?"; its next event revives it. Which pid each harness gives is unverified until the herdr trial (#332): Claude Code's hook is its child, possibly through a shell; agy's wrapper `$PPID` is agy only if agy starts the wrapper itself. A harness whose recorded pid is not its agent's takes the wrong path: one that dies at once would end its sessions wrongly, one that outlives the agent would never end them, so the trial must confirm each. No hook payload carries a pid of its own.

A turn you interrupt with Esc fires no `Stop` and no `idle_prompt`: when its terminal reports OSC 7501 `idle` for 5 seconds while its hooks say it runs, the app appends a `StudioInterrupted` line for that turn, with its end trees, and the turn ends at your turn; outside a studio terminal the next prompt ends it. `done` never ends a turn, as a turn that finished says `done` before its `Stop` lands. A wake-up is not your prompt, so it leaves "needs you" in place. Only "needs you" is in colour. macOS shows a notification when a session newly needs you, a worker fails or it is your turn, while the window is not focused. While it is focused, a session that reaches your turn off screen (not selected and not in the split's second pane) raises a note over the map's top-right corner, in the region named Notifications, under the needs-you toast: its name and its turn's intention ("3 finished" for several within 3 seconds), with the your-turn sound. The note is not in colour, fades after 6 seconds unless the pointer rests on it or the keyboard's focus is in it (it fades 6 seconds after both leave), and a click selects its session; its × hides it, and a newer note replaces it.

The toasts work from the keyboard (#457). The needs-you, note and Clear toasts sit in one region named "Notifications" over the map: the needs-you toast and the note under it down the top-right corner, never overlapping, and the Clear toast in the bottom-left corner. Each is a popover in the top layer, above every panel. Each toast's main action is a button the size of the toast beside its × (for the Clear toast, Undo beside its ×), and Tab reaches each of them; Enter or Space on the needs-you toast jumps to its session and leaves focus mode, as a click does, and on the note selects its session. A toast never takes the focus when it shows, and a press of the pointer on it acts but leaves the keyboard's focus where it was. Closing a toast from the keyboard, by its × or its action, moves the focus to the next toast still shown, else back to where it was before it entered the toasts. A screen reader hears a toast's visible text when it shows or changes, and that it has actions, never its buttons' names. While a modal dialog is open no toast shows; one raised meanwhile shows once the dialog closes.

A subagent's wait, failure or card shows on its session's line and names the subagent; a failed helper raises none. A subagent's name is its spawn's description, or the slash command of the forked skill that started it, such as `/code-review high origin/main...HEAD`, else the first 5 characters of its id; the list, its view's header, the notices and the card writer all use it. A session's folder is the last segment of its `cwd`, or the whole `cwd` when the folder ends in a slash. Notices within 3 seconds of the first show as one notification. The browser asks once for permission to show them, on your first press in the page. The tab's title counts the unseen sessions; it replaced the Dock badge (danielrispler/plotkeeper#331). A blocked command waits on you until the session moves on (#572).

A session with blocked commands waiting shows "N blocked commands waiting" ("1 blocked command waiting") as its row's second line in any state but ended, counting each block until the session moves on: the blocked agent's next call, your next prompt, or a subagent's end. An open question still takes the line. A Bash command auto mode blocks while the app runs raises the notification "Auto mode blocked a command", naming the session and the subagent, under the same rules as the others: not while the window is focused, and combined with notices within 3 seconds. It replaces the "needs you" notice that block would raise. A block replayed at launch raises none.

### Projects

plotkeeper keeps its own list of projects, `projects.json` in the data folder (`server/src/projects.rs`, #381). A repository joins it the first time a session is seen in it, as its main checkout (a task's worktree joins its main checkout's project, the rule the list groups by). It stays after its sessions end or are deleted, and leaves only through Remove project, in the "⋯" menu of a project header with no live session (a project with live sessions would join again at their next event). That menu opens under its button, its end edge at the button's, and flips over the button or to its other side at the window's edges, placed by CSS through the popover's implicit anchor as the rail's "⋯" menu is (#458); a removed project comes back only when a session is active in it after the removal. The list's groups are read from it: every saved project has a header even with no live session, as its header only, after the projects with sessions, in the order they joined. A saved project whose folder was moved or deleted is gone: its header's name is struck through, and in the Map view it shows "This folder is gone", its path and Remove project where its map would be, never an error, and nothing reads its map.

## Home, retired

Home, the waiting room the page once opened on, is retired (#386, #388; [ADR 0001](../../adr/0001-retire-home.md)): the page opens on the map, and nothing draws Home. What it held lives in the list's second lines, Clear landed and the project header's dot.

## Sessions in the studio

The page starts no session: the picker, "+", the project registry ("Add project…"), Workspaces' rows, tasks, branches and "Pull", and the git line are gone from it (danielrispler/plotkeeper#326); sessions start in any terminal and record through the hook.

Cmd+Shift+H, once `studio::GoHome`, is now `studio::ShowMap` (#386), "back to the map", as Cmd+Shift+M is: it closes the left panel, which selects no session when it held a story. A `keymap.json` that still names `studio::GoHome` runs `studio::ShowMap`. A click on the list's empty space does the same. Cmd+J is unchanged.

**A session's name** is written once, from your first prompt (for a bare slash command, from the turn's final response too), by the writer's `claude -p` (`write_name` in `understanding/src/summary.rs`). Its rules ask for a title of at most 5 words and 36 characters, in its own words: not the start of the prompt. Code keeps the reply whole and never cuts it at a word count; only a runaway reply over 60 characters is cut at the last space before 60 and ends in "…" before it is stored (#444). That bound guards the session list, the rail's header and the notifications from a writer that ignores its rules; it is not how a name fits a line, which CSS does with an ellipsis. Your own rename wins over the written name.

"Rename" names the session: the server appends a `StudioRename` line to the event log, so the name survives a restart; the page types nothing into the session. An empty name gives the row its folder's again. "Open" and "Close", which resumed and ended a session's terminal, are gone with the terminals.

"Delete" asks, then drops the session's row: the server appends a `StudioDelete` line to the event log, so the row stays gone after a restart until a `SessionStart` of the session brings it back; its cards stay in the store, and no worktree or branch is removed. Clear landed, at the list's end, deletes the landed rows the same way once its Undo window has passed.

An agy session is recorded from any terminal, not only a studio one: this reverses #43's decision 9, which dropped an agy event that had no `PLOTKEEPER_TERMINAL_ID` because agy's plugin is global. Its place is `PLOTKEEPER_INTERNAL=1`: plotkeeper's own model runs (the outside review's agy, the card writer's `claude -p`) set it, and the hook drops every event from a process that carries it (#323). A session without a studio terminal has no terminal to link, so it shows as a plain session.
