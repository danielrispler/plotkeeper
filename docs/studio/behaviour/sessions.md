# The sessions

## The session list

On the right, the session list: every session (Claude Code, agy and Codex), one line each with the agent's logo, its name and its state, grouped by its project, and, inside a repository, by state with "needs you" first. The list holds what Home showed (#382): a row that needs you adds a second line, the open question's first line, else who asks (a plan, a permission, or a subagent: its name and "asks"); a running row its Intention, or its current step until the Intention is known; an unseen row (your turn or idle, not landed) its last turn's Headline, which updates when the turn's summary is stored. No other row has a second line. A second line is one line, muted, at 11.5 px, cut with an ellipsis. **Cut text reads in full in place** (spec #582): a row's name, token and second line, like every text the layout cuts, keep their whole text in the page (find-in-page, copy and screen readers get all of it), and on hover, or focus on the control that holds them, the cut line scrolls its hidden end into view inside its own box (`cut()` in `ui/dom.ts`, the `.cut` class). A line that fits stays still, and with reduced motion asked for nothing moves. A text the layout shortens (a path) or that can be very long (a prompt) has a hint holding it whole instead. A prompt-named session's name has a hint of its own, its full name and its identity; the row's hint reads its name and state, as every row's does. The selected row adds how long it has waited ("Bash cargo test · 3m"); a session whose last step is a wake-up reads "worker … returned" until its next step. The agent's logo is 12 px in one muted tone; colour in the list is the "Needs you" pill only. **The brief mark** (#630): a live session whose PR has a waiting PR brief (the rail's "PR brief ready" row's rule, `briefs_waiting`; [The rail](rail.md)) carries one quiet mark on its row, the project dot's mist with no colour, in its own 24 px button beside the row's delete, as a button holds no button. Its hint reads "PR #N · brief waiting", and pressing it opens the Map view on that brief, as the rail's row does, without selecting the row (`briefMarkFor` in `ui/rail-brief.ts`). It goes once the brief is read to its end at the PR's head and comes back after a push; an ended session is in History, not the list, and has none, so the Map view's tray stays its way in; the strip has none. Hovering a session's row makes it the spotlit session on the map: its footprint, its chip on its latest turn's parts, and the other session badges dimmed ([The Map view](map.md), "Session badges"). A session row, like every button and link of the session list and the top bar, explains itself in a **hint** (spec #582), a styled hover that replaces the native `title`: it shows when the pointer rests on the control for half a second, or when Tab reaches it, at once while another hint is open, anywhere on the page; it sits above the control and flips below when the window's top is in the way, except in the session list and its strip, where it opens to the control's left, level with it, so it covers no row above or below (spec #658; `hint(trigger, text, "start")`, flipping to the right when the window's edge is in the way); it stays open while the pointer is on it, and closes on Esc. A row's hint reads its name and state, and adds that it touched no part in the last 7 days when it did not; it is built when the row is drawn, and the sidebar is drawn again when the map places the sessions. A hint that adds to the control's name is its accessible description; one that only repeats it adds nothing. `hint(trigger, text)` in `ui/dom.ts` makes one. A fold-out header (`<summary>`), an input or a select, which the browser's own hint wiring cannot reach, shows its hint the same way, by the helper's own pointer and focus listeners (the same delays, the pointer may rest on the hint, Esc and leaving close it, focus never moves into it); a click on a fold-out header still folds it. A button that cannot be used right now (Ask while it waits for its answer, the question card's Send before every question has a choice, a micro-world's "Building…") is `aria-disabled="true"`, not `disabled`: it stays focusable, is announced as unavailable, ignores a click and Enter, and its hint says why. **Marks and dots** (a row's check mark and id mark, a project's mist dot, an agent's logo, a part's law marks and broken-law mark, the sheet's law marks, a stale part's band, a link's law mark, the story's failed-check mark and a clock) show their hint on the pointer alone (`mark()` in `ui/dom.ts`, and `hint()` on a span or an SVG shape): each is `role="img"` with an `aria-label` carrying its meaning, and none takes the Tab order, so what a mark says also stays reachable without a pointer, in the part sheet's law list and the row's own words. **No native `title` is left on the page** outside the terminal, an SVG `<title>` included; the micro-world's frame is named by its `aria-label`. The snap pages keep it so: the hover rule (`scripts/snap/hover.mjs`, #220, rewritten by spec #582) runs after every snap page and fails on a control (`button`, `summary`, `a[href]`, `[role=button]`, the toasts, the spine) with no hint of its own (an ancestor's hover no longer counts), on any `title` attribute or SVG `<title>`, on a hint that only repeats its control's label, and on a hinted element the pointer cannot reach.

**The list holds its order under the pointer** (spec #659). While the pointer is in the list (the full list, the list open over the map, its strip, or the project "⋯" menu), the project groups and the rows in each keep the places of the last draw before it came in, and the strip's buttons theirs: a session that goes from running to needs you keeps its row and its state group, and only its glyph, second line and counts change, as do a header's rollup and dot. A session that starts meanwhile joins the end of its project's group (a new project the end of the groups, a session new to the strip its end), and one no longer listed leaves at once, so a row pressed is always a listed session. A row held in the folded Idle group stays in it, and a strip button whose session went idle stays, until the pointer leaves. When it leaves, the list and the strip are drawn once in the real order. The server's sort and sends are unchanged: the hold is the page's (`holdGroups` in `ui/projects.ts`, `holdList` in `ui/list.ts`). ⌘] and ⌘[, ⌘J and the jump to an unseen session step through the order on screen, held or not, as ↑ and ↓ do; a session Clear landed hid is not in it. The strip's buttons follow the sidebar's order, project by project and by state, held or not, never the server's, so ⌘] and ⌘[ at the strip's width step through them as they show. A hint of something in the list (a row's, a project dot's, which opens over the rows) is drawn outside `.list`, and the pointer on it is still in the list: the hold ends when it leaves the hint for somewhere else, also once the hint's row is gone (its session ended under it). The keyboard in the list holds nothing. The snap `listHeld` checks it with synthetic pointer events.

"Clear landed", quiet under "History · N", shows only while a landed session is in the list (#244, #382). It hides every landed row at once, ended ones included, from the list and the strip, and shows a toast, "Cleared N landed", with Undo for 8 seconds. Undo brings the rows back and nothing was deleted. When the 8 seconds pass, or the app quits inside them, each hidden session goes through Delete: its row drops, and its cards stay. The rows stay hidden until the sessions leave the list, so they never flash back before the delete lands, and a Clear in that gap does not commit them twice; a delete that fails brings its row back. A reload of the page forgets a pending clear. A session that stops being landed inside the window is left alone. The server hears each session until its delete is written, so a quit while the deletes are in flight still commits them. A second Clear inside the window adds its rows and restarts the 8 seconds.

Idle sessions collapse at the bottom; ended ones are in History, from the "History · N" link at the list's end or the palette's "Show history". The rest of the page is the map of a project (see [The Map view](map.md#the-map-view), #386): the session you click opens its story (see [The story rail](rail.md)) in the left panel over its project's map, and History, a turn's whole diff, a subagent's log and Settings open in the same panel. Only your click changes the session shown.

The palette (⌘⇧P; Ctrl+Shift+Space on Linux) lists every action with its keys, filtered by what you type; ↑ and ↓ move the active row, which a screen reader announces, as the filter is a combobox over a listbox of options, and Enter or a click runs one. It is a native popover with a visible × (named "Close"): Esc, a click outside it, its × or ⌘⇧P again close it, and every close puts the focus back where it was before it opened (#458).

A session is running from a prompt until its turn ends; needs you while it waits on your permission, answer or plan approval, or on a command auto mode blocked until the session moves on (see [Blocked commands](cards.md#blocked-commands)); your turn when its turn ended without asking you, or it started and has had no prompt yet; idle after 2 hours at your turn with no events, until its next event; ended from its `SessionEnd` until it starts again under its id. A session also ends when its agent is gone (#330): the hook records `studio_pid` on each event, the pid of its parent, or its parent's parent when that is a shell (`sh`, `ash`, `bash`, `zsh`, `dash`, `fish`), and agy's wrapper passes its own parent as `PLOTKEEPER_AGENT_PID`, which wins. The hook finds each process's executable path and parent by `ps` on macOS and by `/proc` on Linux (`/proc/<pid>/exe`, with a ` (deleted)` suffix dropped, and the parent from `/proc/<pid>/stat`; for an executable named `busybox`, whose one binary is every applet, the applet's name from `stat` stands in, so busybox's `sh` is a shell), since Linux's `ps -o comm` gives only a bare name, which would hide Codex's shared daemon and the IDEs from the checks below (#702). A process of an IDE gives no pid: on macOS one inside an app bundle (`.app/Contents/`), on Linux a VS Code or Cursor under `/usr/share/code/`, `/usr/share/code-insiders/`, `/usr/share/cursor/`, `/usr/lib/code/`, `/opt/Cursor/`, `/opt/cursor/`, `/opt/visual-studio-code/`, `/opt/VSCode…`, a snap (`/snap/code/`, `/snap/code-insiders/`, `/snap/cursor/`), an AppImage mount (`/tmp/.mount_cursor…/cursor`, `/tmp/.mount_code…/code`), or the remote server that Remote-SSH and Remote-WSL install in the home (`~/.vscode-server/`, `~/.cursor-server/`; unverified: no real Remote-SSH or Remote-WSL session has been checked, and the paths come from those servers' install layout). Cursor's renamed extension host, whose title hides its path on macOS, shows its install path on Linux, so on Linux its sessions take the idle path too. About every 30 seconds the server checks each session that has not ended and has a pid (a signal 0 to it, which sends nothing), and appends a `SessionEnd` with reason `process_gone` for each whose process is gone, so a Ctrl+C or a closed herdr pane ends it within about 30 seconds. The lines the server writes itself (this `SessionEnd`, a closed terminal's, an interrupted turn's `StudioInterrupted`) are shaped like Claude Code's whatever the session's agent, so they never change its agent: an agy session ended or interrupted this way keeps agy's logo and transcript reader, and a restart replays it the same (#352). A session with no pid never ends this way: with no events for 2 hours, in a turn or waiting on Daniel as well as at your turn, it goes idle and its row says "Idle?"; its next event revives it. Which pid each harness gives is unverified until the herdr trial (#332): Claude Code's hook is its child, possibly through a shell; agy's wrapper `$PPID` is agy only if agy starts the wrapper itself. A harness whose recorded pid is not its agent's takes the wrong path: one that dies at once would end its sessions wrongly, one that outlives the agent would never end them, so the trial must confirm each. No hook payload carries a pid of its own.

A turn you interrupt with Esc fires no `Stop` and no `idle_prompt`, so your next prompt ends it; a `StudioInterrupted` line (the hook's for a Codex `Interrupt`, or an older log's) ends it at once. A wake-up is not your prompt, so it leaves "needs you" in place. Only "needs you" is in colour. macOS shows a notification when a session newly needs you, a worker fails or it is your turn, while the window is not focused. While it is focused, a session that reaches your turn off screen (not selected) raises a note over the map's top-right corner, in the region named Notifications, under the needs-you toast: its name and its turn's intention ("3 finished" for several within 3 seconds), with the your-turn sound. The note is not in colour, fades after 6 seconds unless the pointer rests on it or the keyboard's focus is in it (it fades 6 seconds after both leave), and a click selects its session; its × hides it, and a newer note replaces it.

The toasts work from the keyboard (#457). The needs-you, note and Clear toasts sit in one region named "Notifications" over the map: the needs-you toast and the note under it down the top-right corner, never overlapping, and the Clear toast in the bottom-left corner. While the map's side area shows (the not placed list, a part's sheet, its drawer, the runs), the needs-you, note, copy and what's-new toasts stack down from the map's top against the side area's left edge instead, less the region's margin, so they cover neither the not placed line nor the sheet's header and ×; they move again whenever the side area shows, hides or is resized (#666). Each is a popover in the top layer, above every panel. Each toast's main action is a button the size of the toast beside its × (for the Clear toast, Undo beside its ×), and Tab reaches each of them; Enter or Space on the needs-you toast jumps to its session and leaves focus mode, as a click does, and on the note selects its session. A toast never takes the focus when it shows, and a press of the pointer on it acts but leaves the keyboard's focus where it was. Closing a toast from the keyboard, by its × or its action, moves the focus to the next toast still shown, else back to where it was before it entered the toasts. A screen reader hears a toast's visible text when it shows or changes, and that it has actions, never its buttons' names. While a modal dialog is open no toast shows; one raised meanwhile shows once the dialog closes.

A subagent's wait, failure or card shows on its session's line and names the subagent; a failed helper raises none. A subagent's name is its spawn's description, or the slash command of the forked skill that started it, such as `/code-review high origin/main...HEAD`, else the first 5 characters of its id; the list, its view's header, the notices and the card writer all use it. A session's folder is the last segment of its `cwd`, or the whole `cwd` when the folder ends in a slash. Notices within 3 seconds of the first show as one notification. The browser asks once for permission to show them, on your first press in the page. The tab's title counts the unseen sessions; it replaced the Dock badge (danielrispler/plotkeeper#331). A blocked command waits on you until the session moves on (#572).

A session with blocked commands waiting shows "N blocked commands waiting" ("1 blocked command waiting") as its row's second line in any state but ended, counting each block until the session moves on: the blocked agent's next call, your next prompt, or a subagent's end. An open question still takes the line. A Bash command auto mode blocks while the app runs raises the notification "Auto mode blocked a command", naming the session and the subagent, under the same rules as the others: not while the window is focused, and combined with notices within 3 seconds. It replaces the "needs you" notice that block would raise. A block replayed at launch raises none.

### Projects

plotkeeper keeps its own list of projects, `projects.json` in the data folder (`server/src/projects.rs`, #381). A repository joins it the first time a session is seen in it, as the session's project, unless it is temporary. It stays after its sessions end or are deleted, and leaves only through Remove project, in the "⋯" menu of a project header with no live session (a project with live sessions would join again at their next event). That menu opens under its button, its end edge at the button's, and flips over the button or to its other side at the window's edges, placed by CSS through the popover's implicit anchor as the rail's "⋯" menu is (#458); a removed project comes back only when a session is active in it after the removal. The list's groups are read from it: every saved project has a header even with no live session, as its header only, after the projects with sessions, in the order they joined, except a gone or temporary one, which has a header only while it has a live session (a session Clear landed hid counts). **A project's header has two controls** (spec #659, #674). Its chevron, before the name and shown only while the group has live sessions, folds and unfolds the group: it is named "Sessions of <project>", carries `aria-expanded` as the subagent chevrons do, and its hint reads "Fold or unfold its sessions"; the fold is kept across updates. Its name always shows that project's map, the shown project's included (a session's panel of another project closes, as before); it has no `aria-expanded`, and its hint keeps the project's path and says "Show its map". A saved project with no live session shows no chevron. The snap `projectChevron` checks it, and the a11y pass checks its name. A saved project whose folder was moved or deleted is gone: the list hides it while it has no live session, and with one its header's name is struck through; the server still lists it, so its header comes back on its own when its folder exists again. In the Map view, opened on it as the last viewed project or from a live session's header, it shows "This folder is gone", its path and Remove project where its map would be, never an error, and nothing reads its map.

**Temporary folders** (spec #659): a folder under `/tmp`, `/private/tmp`, `/var/folders` or `/private/var/folders` (macOS's per-user temporary folder lives under the last two) is temporary, so scratchpads and trial repos never pile up. The server never saves a new one, and lists one an earlier build saved marked temporary, as it marks gone ones; `projects.json` is not rewritten for it, so it too leaves only through Remove project. A live session in a temporary folder is still listed, under its folder's header, until it ends.

**A session's project** (spec #659) is set once, from the first folder it gets (its first own event with a folder): that folder's git top level, else the folder itself when it is not in git, and for a task's worktree `<main>/.claude/worktrees/<task>` its main checkout `<main>`. The core keeps it on the session (`project`, stored with it: [storage](storage.md)), and a `cd` never changes it: a session started in `plotkeeper` that runs `cd ui` stays in plotkeeper, while its folder, folder name and worktree name keep following its events. A session that starts at home and `cd`s into a repository stays in its starting folder's project. The list's groups, the map's choice of project for a selected session, the selection the page opens with, History's project, the map's session badges, the rail's PR brief row, the saved project list and the PR lookups all read it; nothing derives a project from a session's folder.

## Home, retired

Home, the waiting room the page once opened on, is retired (#386, #388; [ADR 0001](../../adr/0001-retire-home.md)): the page opens on the map, and nothing draws Home. What it held lives in the list's second lines, Clear landed and the project header's dot.

## Sessions in the studio

The page starts no session: the picker, "+", the project registry ("Add project…"), Workspaces' rows, tasks, branches and "Pull", and the git line are gone from it (danielrispler/plotkeeper#326); sessions start in any terminal and record through the hook.

Cmd+Shift+H, once `studio::GoHome`, is now `studio::ShowMap` (#386), "back to the map", as Cmd+Shift+M is: it closes the left panel, which selects no session when it held a story. A `keymap.json` that still names `studio::GoHome` runs `studio::ShowMap`. A click on the list's empty space does the same. Cmd+J is unchanged.

**A session's name** is written once, from your first prompt (for a bare slash command, from the turn's final response too), by the writer's `claude -p` (`write_name` in `understanding/src/summary.rs`). Its rules ask for a title of at most 5 words and 36 characters, in its own words: not the start of the prompt. Code keeps the reply whole and never cuts it at a word count; only a runaway reply over 60 characters is cut at the last space before 60 and ends in "…" before it is stored (#444). That bound guards the session list, the rail's header and the notifications from a writer that ignores its rules; it is not how a name fits a line, which CSS does with an ellipsis. Your own rename wins over the written name.

"Rename" names the session: the server appends a `StudioRename` line to the event log, so the name survives a restart; the page types nothing into the session. An empty name gives the row its folder's again. "Open" and "Close", which resumed and ended a session's terminal, are gone with the terminals.

"Delete" asks, then drops the session's row: the server appends a `StudioDelete` line to the event log, so the row stays gone after a restart until a `SessionStart` of the session brings it back; its cards stay in the store, and no worktree or branch is removed. Clear landed, at the list's end, deletes the landed rows the same way once its Undo window has passed.

An agy session is recorded from any terminal: this reverses #43's decision 9, which dropped an agy event that had no studio terminal because agy's plugin is global. Its place is `PLOTKEEPER_INTERNAL=1`: plotkeeper's own model runs (the outside review's agy, the card writer's `claude -p`) set it, and the hook drops every event from a process that carries it (#323). agy's payloads name no parent conversation, so an agy subagent shows as a session of its own (#725).
