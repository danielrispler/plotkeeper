# The sessions

## The session list

On the right, the session list: every session (Claude Code, agy and Codex), one line each with the agent's logo, its name and its state, grouped by repository, a task's worktree under its main checkout's, and, inside a repository, by state with "needs you" first. A running row adds a second line, its current step, and a row that needs you the open question's first line, else who asks (a plan, a permission, or a subagent: its name and "asks"), and the selected row adds how long it has waited ("Bash cargo test · 3m"); a session whose last step is a wake-up reads "worker … returned" until its next step.

Idle sessions collapse at the bottom; ended ones are in History, from the "History · N" link at the list's end or the palette's "Show history". On the left, the story rail of the session you clicked (see [The story rail](rail.md)); the centre holds Home, History, a turn's whole diff or a subagent's log, and, with a session selected and none of those, a line pointing at the rail. Only your click changes the session shown.

A session is running from a prompt until its turn ends; needs you while it waits on your permission, answer, plan approval or click on a blocked command (see [Blocked commands](cards.md#blocked-commands)); your turn when its turn ended without asking you, or it started and has had no prompt yet; idle after 2 hours at your turn with no events, until its next event; ended from its `SessionEnd` until it starts again under its id. A turn you interrupt with Esc fires no `Stop` and no `idle_prompt`: when its terminal reports OSC 7501 `idle` for 5 seconds while its hooks say it runs, the app appends a `StudioInterrupted` line for that turn, with its end trees, and the turn ends at your turn; outside a studio terminal the next prompt ends it. `done` never ends a turn, as a turn that finished says `done` before its `Stop` lands. A wake-up is not your prompt, so it leaves "needs you" in place. Only "needs you" is in colour. macOS shows a notification when a session newly needs you, a card arrives, a worker fails or it is your turn, while the window is not focused. While it is focused, a session that reaches your turn off screen (not selected and not in the split's second pane) raises a note over the terminal's top-right corner, under the needs-you toast: its name and its turn's intention ("3 finished" for several within 3 seconds), with the your-turn sound. The note is not in colour, fades after 6 seconds unless the pointer rests on it, and a click selects its session; a newer note replaces it. The top bar's unseen chip, beside the needs-you chip, counts the unseen sessions and selects the newest, as ⌘⇧U does.

A session whose context share passes `context_warning` (30% by default) raises the same note, "Context passed 30%: now 34%", with no sound and whether or not it is on screen, and its row's "context N%" turns from muted to full strength until the share falls back to the threshold or under. It warns once per crossing. A session already past it when the app starts raises no note; a changed threshold re-arms every session, so each one past the new threshold warns once.

A subagent's wait, failure or card shows on its session's line and names the subagent; a failed helper raises none. A subagent's name is its spawn's description, or the slash command of the forked skill that started it, such as `/code-review high origin/main...HEAD`, else the first 5 characters of its id; the list, its view's header, the notices and the card writer all use it. A session's folder is the last segment of its `cwd`, or the whole `cwd` when the folder ends in a slash. Notices within 3 seconds of the first show as one notification. macOS asks once for permission to show them. The Dock badge counts the sessions that need you, and a session with a blocked command waiting once, unless it has ended. A blocked command waits while its card has a button: Blocked, Blocked again and Allowed, not run count; an Allowed card, which waits for the agent's retry and not for you, does not.

A session with blocked commands waiting shows "N blocked commands waiting" ("1 blocked command waiting") as its row's second line in any state but ended, counting the cards that have a button (Blocked, Blocked again, Allowed, not run), until each is chosen (or declined). An Allowed card, Running, Finished or Running in background does not count. An open question still takes the line. A Bash command auto mode blocks while the app runs raises the notification "Auto mode blocked a command", naming the session and the subagent, under the same rules as the others: not while the window is focused, and combined with notices within 3 seconds. It replaces the "needs you" notice that block would raise. A block replayed at launch raises none.

## Home

With no session selected the centre column shows Home, the waiting room, as a page of parts in a fixed order under a quiet title (the date). A part with nothing in it is absent: Needs you, Running, Unseen, the learning slot (one fresh Quiz or Retest, or "Done for today"), Landed, and the repositories sessions ran in, one row each, which opens that repository's map.

Needs you lists each session that needs Daniel as a card on the raised surface, with its "Needs you" pill and its question's first line. It is the only colour on the page; with no session needing him Home says "Nothing needs you".

Running shows at most 4 sessions, two lines each: the agent's logo, the session's name, its project and how long its turn has run, then its intention as the rail shows it, muted, with its age in steps. More hide behind "+N running". Unseen shows at most 3 of the your-turn or idle sessions whose last turn Daniel has not seen (a running session is listed as running only), newest turn first, one line each with the turn's Headline, then "+N unseen". A row leaves once its turn is seen, whether or not he replies. Clicking a "+N" row shows the rest in place, until Home is left; drawing Home again while it is on screen (a list click on its empty space, a new reply from the store) keeps those groups, a reveal and "Not now". Clicking a row selects its session.

Landed has one "Clear" on its heading, and each row keeps its Delete on hover. Clear hides every landed row at once and shows a toast, "Cleared N landed", with Undo for 8 seconds. Undo brings the rows back and nothing was deleted. When the 8 seconds pass, or the app quits inside them, each hidden session goes through Delete: its terminal ends and its row drops, and its cards stay, so its Retests still come. The rows stay hidden until the sessions leave the list, so they never flash back before the delete lands, and a Clear in that gap does not commit them twice; a delete that fails brings its row back. A quit also ends the terminals' sessions as the window's own commit does, and a reload of the window forgets a pending clear. A session that stops being landed inside the window is left alone. The shell hears each session until its delete is written, so a quit while the deletes are in flight still commits them; at quit it checks each again (still landed, the terminal it runs in now), ends each terminal once, and never ends one that a session not being cleared still runs in (the window's own commit does the same). A second Clear inside the window adds its rows and restarts the 8 seconds.

Landed keeps each row's Delete, and the project rows work as before. Drawing is patched by key, so focus survives an update.

## Sessions in the studio

The page starts no session: the picker, "+", the project registry ("Add project…"), Workspaces' rows, tasks, branches and "Pull", and the git line are gone from it (danielrispler/plotkeeper#326); sessions start in any terminal and record through the hook.

Cmd+Shift+H (`studio::GoHome`, rebindable) flips between Home and the session you came from, as tmux's `last-window` does: from any view but Home (a session, History, a full diff, a new terminal) it shows Home, and every way to Home (this key, a click on the list's empty space, a Delete) remembers the session left; on Home it selects the remembered session if the list still holds it, and does nothing if it is gone. Cmd+J is unchanged, and Home never switches the view by itself.

"Rename" names the session: the server appends a `StudioRename` line to the event log, so the name survives a restart; the page types nothing into the session. An empty name gives the row its folder's again. "Open" and "Close", which resumed and ended a session's terminal, are gone with the terminals.

"Delete" asks, then drops the session's row: the server appends a `StudioDelete` line to the event log, so the row stays gone after a restart until a `SessionStart` of the session brings it back; its cards stay in the store, and no worktree or branch is removed. Clear on Home deletes the landed rows the same way once its Undo window has passed.

A late Allow of a blocked command reaches no terminal from the page: it shows the line to give the session to retry the command.

An agy session is recorded from any terminal, not only a studio one: this reverses #43's decision 9, which dropped an agy event that had no `STUDIO_TERMINAL_ID` because agy's plugin is global. Its place is `PLOTKEEPER_INTERNAL=1`: plotkeeper's own model runs (the outside review's agy, the card writer's `claude -p`) set it, and the hook drops every event from a process that carries it (#323). A session without a studio terminal has no terminal to link, so it shows as a plain session.
