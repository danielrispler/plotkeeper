# Blocked commands, wake-ups and what a turn changed

Cards, which explained what a prompt or a worker changed, are retired (danielrispler/plotkeeper#348): a PR's brief is the explanation now ([PR briefs](pr-briefs.md)), and a turn is told by its Headline and Body ([the rail](rail.md)). The cards written before stay in the store and still load ([storage](storage.md)), and none is shown. This file keeps the other cards: a blocked command's, below, and the order the app writes in.

**Turn writing** is the order the app writes in. A turn's end asks for the turn summary and the session's Goal, a worker's return for the worker's Goal, and the first prompt of a session for its name. Each session's pieces are written one at a time, in the order they were asked for, while sessions write in parallel with each other, so one session's slow writer call holds no other's. A turn's end writes its summary and the session's Goal (again if a prompt of yours came), then Design so far when its sources changed, and no card. The summary is written when it is wanted: a final reply of 150 words or more, or one that ends in a question, or a turn that ends asking you something; a turn that changed code and ended with a short reply gets no summary, and its row shows the first line of the agent's last message. When summaries are written on open, the turn waits for you, and opening it writes the summary and shows it as soon as it is written, with Design so far written after it. A turn you open in an ended session, from History, gets its summary and its Design so far the same way. A session deleted while a job waited gets nothing. The writer reads the session's workers and your prompts as they are when it is written.

To tell what a prompt changed, the app fingerprints the session's git worktree at its prompt and at its `Stop`: `HEAD`, and the files `git status` lists, with their content. The change is the difference: the files listed either time, and the files the commits in between changed. Each change's end is the next one's base, and each session keeps its own, so another session's prompt in the same worktree moves no base. A worktree made after the turn or the worker began has no earlier record: its base is the commit it was created at, the first line of its reflog, with no changed files, so an edit made before its first fingerprint counts. A worktree that was already there takes its files at the first look as its base. The hook's start tree for such a worktree follows the same rule. A prompt that lands while the session's turn is still open, after an interrupt, takes no new base, so the interrupted prompt's change stays in its turn. A session that changed nothing itself has no change at its `Stop`, whatever its workers wrote. A turn's facts, its files and checks, include its workers'. A file a helper, or another subagent with no worktree of its own, edits or writes in its parent's worktree shows under that subagent's name.

On any step of a worker, or of a prompt of yours before its turn ends, when 20 minutes or more have passed since the last look at that worktree, or since its last such look that found no change, the app looks again, and a look that finds no change starts a new 20-minute wait. A worker is also looked at after each Bash step of its own, taking a new base only when its `HEAD` moved, and at its end (`SubagentStop`, or for a killed worker, which has none, the wake-up that reports it).

A call touches the worktree of its folder and, for an `Edit` or `Write`, of its file's path. A Bash call also touches each worktree of the repository whose absolute path its command names: the hook lists them on the event (`studio_touched_roots`). A path counts for the deepest worktree that holds it.

A subagent's home is the worktree it works in. An isolated worker's is the one it reports. A subagent that was not spawned isolated reports its session's folder, so its home is the first worktree its calls touch that is not its session's: the app fingerprints it there from that call on. Its later touch of another worktree does not move its home. A subagent that touches no other worktree stays in its session's. It is a helper until a fingerprint in its home finds a change, then a worker; while a worker runs, its session's own touches skip its home.

Each subagent gets its own fingerprint too, of the folder of its first event: its base. A helper is also fingerprinted right before each Bash step: a change across that step makes it a worker, while what others changed in a shared worktree before it does not. After a restart the app fingerprints the turns still open and the subagents still running, so their next look can be compared.

Ignored files do not count, and a commit alone changes nothing. A file over 1 MiB is compared by its size and modification time, and the writer gets one line that names it instead of its diff.

The writer's limits are in [PR briefs](pr-briefs.md#the-writers-limits): it runs `claude -p` on your subscription with no tools and no MCP servers, and it gets at most the first 64 KiB of what it reads.

When the writer cannot write, `plotkeeper`'s page shows one line on its notice line, starting "Summaries are off:", with why and what to do, each writer with its own fix: not logged in (log in to `claude`, or to `agy` for agy), quota or rate limit (wait, or pick another model or writer in Settings), timeout, not installed, or an empty or failed reply (run it by hand to see why). The line shows once for each reason, replaces itself when the reason changes and clears when a call works; a page opened later asks for it (`writer_notice`). The log keeps only that one arrived or cleared, never what the writer printed. A call kept short on purpose, the risk judge's, timing out raises none, and neither does the writer section's Test, which shows its outcome in the section. PR briefs are written by `claude` whatever the writer, so the line speaks of summaries only. The pull-request lookups run in the `plotkeeper` process, on a turn's end, the page's focus and the Map view's opening, never on a timer.

## Wake-ups

When a subagent or a background command returns, Claude Code sends its parent a prompt that names the task's id in markup: `<agent-message from="…">` or `<task-notification><task-id>…</task-id>`. A prompt that names a task id the session knows, from a spawn's response, a background command's response or `Stop`'s `background_tasks`, that way is a wake-up, never your prompt. The session's row and its view's "now" line read "worker … returned", with the subagent's name or else the task's short id, until the session's own next step or your next prompt; its last step stays.

A turn a wake-up opened that changed nothing itself writes only its summary and the session's Goal, which reads your prompts and never the wake-up. A known id in plain text ("check what a1191 did") is your prompt. A known id in any other markup is your prompt too, logs one line in the Studio log and counts in `weekly`: Claude Code may have changed its markup.

## Blocked commands

In a Claude Code session in auto mode, a Bash command that auto mode blocks is handled the regular way, in Daniel's IDE or agent: plotkeeper shows no card for it and gives no Allow (#572). The plugin's `PermissionDenied` hook only records the event, and the answer hook returns no decision at once, so the agent's own UI handles the denial. A block of any other tool keeps Claude Code's own notice, and Codex and agy keep their own prompts.

The session reads as waiting on Daniel: it needs you, on its row and its badge on the map, and its row's second line reads "1 blocked command waiting" (see [Sessions](sessions.md)). It waits until the session moves on: the blocked agent's next call, Daniel's next prompt, or, for a subagent's block, that subagent's end. The turn ending does not move it on, since the agent stopped for Daniel. A blocked command the session moved on from reads Moved on, and counts no more.

A log from before #572 still replays as it was: an Allow or Decline given on the old card enters the session's decision history, with the risk note (danielrispler/plotkeeper#298) kept for it in `risk-notes.json` beside the answer: the note's level and line when it arrived before the answer, "answered before the risk note arrived" when it arrived after (or in the same millisecond, or with no time kept), and "no risk note" when the command has none. A question, a plan, a permission for another tool or an ordinary Bash permission shows no such line. Nothing asks for new risk notes.
