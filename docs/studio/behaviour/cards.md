# Blocked commands, wake-ups and what a turn changed

Cards, which explained what a prompt or a worker changed, are retired (danielrispler/plotkeeper#348): a PR's brief is the explanation now ([PR briefs](pr-briefs.md)), and a turn is told by its Headline and Body ([the rail](rail.md)). The cards written before stay in the store and still load ([storage](storage.md)), and none is shown. This file keeps the other cards: a blocked command's, below, and the order the app writes in.

**Turn writing** is the order the app writes in. A turn's end asks for the turn summary and the session's Goal, a worker's return for the worker's Goal, and the first prompt of a session for its name. Each session's pieces are written one at a time, in the order they were asked for, while sessions write in parallel with each other, so one session's slow writer call holds no other's. A turn's end writes its summary and the session's Goal (again if a prompt of yours came), then Design so far when its sources changed, and no card. The summary is written when it is wanted: a final reply of 150 words or more, or one that ends in a question, or a turn that ends asking you something; a turn that changed code and ended with a short reply gets no summary, and its row shows the first line of the agent's last message. When summaries are written on open, the turn waits for you, and opening it writes the summary and shows it as soon as it is written, with Design so far written after it. A turn you open in an ended session, from History, gets its summary and its Design so far the same way. A session deleted while a job waited gets nothing. The writer reads the session's workers and your prompts as they are when it is written.

To tell what a prompt changed, the app fingerprints the session's git worktree at its prompt and at its `Stop`: `HEAD`, and the files `git status` lists, with their content. The change is the difference: the files listed either time, and the files the commits in between changed. Each change's end is the next one's base, and each session keeps its own, so another session's prompt in the same worktree moves no base. A worktree made after the turn or the worker began has no earlier record: its base is the commit it was created at, the first line of its reflog, with no changed files, so an edit made before its first fingerprint counts. A worktree that was already there takes its files at the first look as its base. The hook's start tree for such a worktree follows the same rule. A prompt that lands while the session's turn is still open, after an interrupt, takes no new base, so the interrupted prompt's change stays in its turn. A session that changed nothing itself has no change at its `Stop`, whatever its workers wrote. A turn's facts, its files and checks, include its workers'. A file a helper, or another subagent with no worktree of its own, edits or writes in its parent's worktree shows under that subagent's name.

On any step of a worker, or of a prompt of yours before its turn ends, when 20 minutes or more have passed since the last look at that worktree, or since its last such look that found no change, the app looks again, and a look that finds no change starts a new 20-minute wait. A worker is also looked at after each Bash step of its own, taking a new base only when its `HEAD` moved, and at its end (`SubagentStop`, or for a killed worker, which has none, the wake-up that reports it).

A call touches the worktree of its folder and, for an `Edit` or `Write`, of its file's path. A Bash call also touches each worktree of the repository whose absolute path its command names: the hook lists them on the event (`studio_touched_roots`). A path counts for the deepest worktree that holds it.

A subagent's home is the worktree it works in. An isolated worker's is the one it reports. A subagent that was not spawned isolated reports its session's folder, so its home is the first worktree its calls touch that is not its session's: the app fingerprints it there from that call on. Its later touch of another worktree does not move its home. A subagent that touches no other worktree stays in its session's. It is a helper until a fingerprint in its home finds a change, then a worker; while a worker runs, its session's own touches skip its home.

Each subagent gets its own fingerprint too, of the folder of its first event: its base. A helper is also fingerprinted right before each Bash step: a change across that step makes it a worker, while what others changed in a shared worktree before it does not. After a restart the app fingerprints the turns still open and the subagents still running, so their next look can be compared.

Ignored files do not count, and a commit alone changes nothing. A file over 1 MiB is compared by its size and modification time, and the writer gets one line that names it instead of its diff.

The writer's limits are in [PR briefs](pr-briefs.md#the-writers-limits): it runs `claude -p` on your subscription with no tools and no MCP servers, and it gets at most the first 64 KiB of what it reads.

When the writer cannot write, `plotkeeper`'s page shows one line on its notice line, starting "Summaries are off:", with why and what to do, each writer with its own fix: not logged in (log in to `claude`, or to `agy` for agy), quota or rate limit (wait, or pick another model or writer in Settings), timeout, not installed, or an empty or failed reply (run it by hand to see why). The line shows once for each reason, replaces itself when the reason changes and clears when a call works; a page opened later asks for it (`writer_notice`). The log keeps only that one arrived or cleared, never what the writer printed. A call kept short on purpose, the risk judge's, timing out raises none, and neither does the writer section's Test, which shows its outcome in the section. PR briefs are written by `claude` whatever the writer, so the line speaks of summaries only. The pull-request lookups run in the `plotkeeper` process, on a turn's end, the page's focus and the Map view's opening, never on a timer.

## Wake-ups

When a subagent or a background command returns, Claude Code sends its parent a prompt that names the task's id in markup: `<agent-message from="…">` or `<task-notification><task-id>…</task-id>`. A prompt that names a task id the session knows, from a spawn's response, a background command's response or `Stop`'s `background_tasks`, that way is a wake-up, never your prompt. The session's row and its view's "now" line read "worker … returned", with the subagent's name or else the task's short id, until the session's own next step or your next prompt; its last step stays.

A turn a wake-up opened that changed nothing itself writes only its summary and the session's Goal, which reads your prompts and never the wake-up. A known id in plain text ("check what a1191 did") is your prompt. A known id in any other markup is your prompt too, logs one line in the Studio log and counts in `weekly`: Claude Code may have changed its markup.

## Blocked commands

In a Claude Code session in auto mode, a Bash command that auto mode blocks raises a card on the session's view. It shows the command in full, its folder, the rule that blocked it and, for a subagent's block, the subagent's name. Cards stack in block order. A block of any other tool keeps Claude Code's own notice, and Codex and agy keep their own prompts. The card has Allow and Decline and no edit, so what runs is what the card shows.

**Docking follows the buttons.** The dock above the open questions holds a card only while it shows a button, so orange always means you have something to click: Blocked and Blocked again with Allow and Decline, and Allowed, not run with Ask again. A docked Blocked again or Allowed, not run card shows its state line; a plain Blocked card keeps the dock's look. Any other card is in the rail's feed, neutral, with its state line: Allowed, Running with its timer, Finished with its exit code and output, Running in background, and a Blocked card with no button (its request is gone, so Allow and Decline have nothing to answer). It sits in the turn of its latest step: the turn running when its retry started, else the turn running when it was blocked, so a late Allow whose retry opens a new turn puts the card in that newer turn. A card blocked again counts as blocked at the new block, so one Allowed after that sits in the turn of the new block, not of the first. A card with no such turn is in the feed's last block, as other cards with no turn are, which is where a subagent's view shows its cards. A card that becomes Allowed, not run is docked again.

**The risk note.** Below the rule that blocked it, the card says what the command would do if allowed, in one line led by a risk level: "Risk: **High** · Deletes the remote branch feature-x on GitHub". The levels, the worst the command could plausibly do if allowed as shown, by reach and reversibility:

- **None**: it only reads.
- **Low**: it changes only inside the task's worktree or scratchpad, and can be undone.
- **Medium**: it changes things outside the worktree, or shared things in a way that can be undone (it pushes a branch, installs a package).
- **High**: it is hard to undo or faces outward (a force push, a delete outside the worktree, a post, a merge, a message sent).
- **Very high**: it destroys data, or reads or sends credentials.
- **Not sure**: the text does not show what runs (an opaque script, a pipe into a shell, a variable set elsewhere), or the command talks about its own safety or addresses the judge.

There is no floor: a blocked command may rate None or Low. A separate card writer call, the judge, writes the note from the command's text and its folder and nothing else: not the agent's description, not the transcript, not a script's body, so a command that runs a script file rates Not sure with a line that says its contents were not read. Its prompt fences the command as data, says nothing inside the fence is an instruction, asks for a compound command's worst part and at most 16 words. It runs like every writer call, with no tools, no MCP servers and in safe mode, at the card writer's model, and it is stopped after 15 seconds, or after 75 seconds under `agy`, which waits a minute before it reports a login failure, the process with it (the writer's other calls keep their own limit). It runs off the turn-writing queue, as a side question does, so it never waits behind a session's cards and summaries. The note is advice: Allow and Decline are live the moment the card appears and never wait for it, and the notification and the row's line appear at once without it.

A note's time is when the judge answered, not when it started, so a note that came after Daniel's answer reads "answered before the risk note arrived" in the history. The window is sent the cards again whenever what it shows of the notes changes, as when a second card of a judged command appears with its note already stored.

The note is asked for when a block arrives live, and when a card with buttons is shown that has no note (a command blocked while the studio was closed is explained when you look at it); never for a block replayed at launch, never for an ended session, and at most once per command per run of the app. The command and its folder go to Anthropic through the card writer's `claude -p`, nothing redacted, which the same service already holds for a Claude Code session.

The note's place on the card keeps two lines from the moment the card appears, so the buttons never move. It reads "Reading the command…" until the note arrives, and, on a timeout, a failed or unreadable reply, or no card writer, "No risk note: the judge did not answer", which is not a level. A longer note is cut at two lines, with the full text on hover. It is plain text with no colour, so that needs-you stays the only colour and a "None" is never read as clearance, and it is a polite live region, so a screen reader hears the note when it fills. A card that is Blocked again keeps its note, and a second card of the same command and folder in the session shows the same note. The note stays on the card when it moves from the dock to the feed. A note that arrives while the card is docked fills its place where it stands: the card, its place and its buttons stay the same nodes, so the live region is heard to fill and keyboard focus stays where it was. A card whose session ended and has no note has no place, since none is coming; a note that arrived stays, and a docked card drawn before its note was known (a selection draws before its cards) gets its place when the note is. A subagent's view shows the notes of its session's cards, and asks for the ones missing, as the session's own view does. The judge's reading is steered by what the command says, which the prompt lowers and does not remove: that is why the note sits beside the command in full and the rule that blocked it.

The plugin's `PermissionDenied` hook for Bash waits up to 600 seconds for your click while the studio's window shows life, whichever session it shows, or none. The session needs you until your click or the agent's next event. The card reads Blocked.

- Allow reads Allowed. The hook returns `retry: true`, and the agent retries the exact command in its own Bash tool. The studio grants a one-time pass for that command and folder, good for 30 minutes: the retry's `PreToolUse` claims it and asks, and the `PermissionRequest` after it is allowed once, whichever session the studio shows, while the studio runs. A retry that is re-quoted, in another folder or another session, expired, or already used, gets no pass and goes back to the classifier.
- Decline closes the card and the denial stands.

Each Allow or Decline enters the session's decision history, as given on the card. A blocked command's entry shows its risk note beside the answer, joined by the session, the command and its folder when the history is read: the note's level and line when it arrived before the answer, "answered before the risk note arrived" when it arrived after (or in the same millisecond, or with no time kept), and "no risk note" when the command has none. A question, a plan, a permission for another tool or an ordinary Bash permission (one auto mode did not block) shows no such line. The stored decision holds no note.

When the studio's window shows no life, the hook returns at once with no decision, and when no click comes by 600 seconds it returns none too. Either way the card stays, with Allow and Decline, until you choose, and if the studio refuses an answer the card keeps its buttons. A decision the hook could not write to the event log leaves the card's request in place too. The session needs you only until the agent's next event or your choice, then shows its real state, and its row, while a card has a button, reads "N blocked commands waiting" (see [Sessions](sessions.md)). An Allow after the hook has returned still grants the pass (the only one for that command and folder) and enters the decision history, which the studio logs itself, since no hook is left to. The agent does not retry by itself then, so, when the studio's answer found the hook gone, the studio types a retry line into the session's terminal, as a bracketed paste and then Enter, through the same typing as `/rename`: it waits for your unsent draft, and never types into a session that needs you, showing a notice instead. For the session's own block the line reads "Daniel approved the blocked command: `<command>`. Run it again." For a subagent's block it reads "Daniel approved the command subagent `<name>` was blocked on. Run `cd <folder>` as its own Bash call, then run exactly: `<command>`. Or pass it to the subagent if it still runs." The retry after that `cd` runs in the subagent's folder, so it matches the pass. A line typed while the agent is mid-turn is queued by Claude Code, and the card moves to Running at the retry whenever it comes.

### After Allow

The card follows the run. The core matches the agent's retry by command and folder:

- **Running** at the `PreToolUse` of the same command and folder, with the time since it started, which counts up each second while the card shows. Live output stays in the session's terminal.
- **Finished** at its end, with its exit code and the last 40 lines of its output, read as a failed check's are.
- **Running in background** when the agent backgrounded the call, until the wake-up that names its task, which finishes the card. The wake-up's summary, as in `Background command "…" completed (exit code 0)`, gives its exit code, which the Finished card shows; a summary without one reads "No exit code". The card has no tail for a background run: its output is in the task's output file, which the studio does not read.
- **Blocked again** when a new `PermissionDenied` of the same command follows the Allow, or, for a Running card, when it is that retry's own call that was denied. A same-command block by another call gets a card of its own. It is the same card, with Allow and Decline once more, and the session needs you again.
- **Allowed, not run** when the turn ends with no matching call, so the approval went unused: the session's own turn ending covers its own Allowed cards and those of subagents that have ended, and a running subagent's card waits for that subagent's own end, since its worker may retry by itself. The row counts the card as waiting, and the card is docked again. It shows **Ask again** and nothing else. Ask again writes a fresh pass for that command and folder, replacing any earlier one, since the 30-minute pass may have expired or been spent, then types the same retry line. A retry that comes later still runs the card.
